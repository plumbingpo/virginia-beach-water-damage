Water Heater Leak Cleanup Help in Virginia Beach

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. The tank is connected to your supply line, so when the bottom rusts through it refills as fast as it empties. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

After extracting the water, our Virginia Beach crew checks the materials that sat next to the unit. Usually the tank platform and the lower part of the closet walls have taken on the most water. When the heater is in the garage, items kept on the slab get wet and so does the wall adjoining the house. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. Each of those areas is metered, then dried using dehumidifiers and air movers.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. On a gas unit, rotate the gas control to its off setting. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. If it is stuck or leaking, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideIf you can reach the drain valve, attach a garden hose and run it outside or to a floor drain so the remaining water does not empty onto the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsLift boxes, rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • A drain valve that loosens, cracks or no longer closes all the way.
  • An overflow pan that cannot drain, because its line is clogged or the pan itself has rusted.
  • Water discharged when the pressure and temperature relief valve fails or opens.
  • Excess pressure in the tank from a failed thermostat or a missing expansion tank.
  • Fittings left slightly loose by recent repair or installation work.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Closet Walls

Water wicks up the drywall around an interior heater closet and into the rooms on the other side. Baseboards swell and the lower wall stays wet inside.

Platform and Subfloor

More water goes into the subfloor or wooden stand beneath the tank than anywhere else. It can rot and weaken, which matters when a new full tank is set on it.

Shared Garage Wall

The wall between the garage and the house takes water into its bottom plate and drywall from the slab, and from there it reaches the floors of the living space.

Ceilings Under an Attic Heater

When an attic heater leaks, water passes through the insulation and lands on the ceiling underneath. Soaked insulation loses its insulating value and gets heavy enough to collapse the drywall ceiling.

Insurance

What About Insurance?

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. If an adjuster finds signs of a long-term leak, such as rust trails or old staining, the claim can be reduced or denied because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Until the claim is closed, hold on to the old heater. Photograph it along with the damage, and review your policy.

Cost

How the Quote Is Built

  • The volume of wet stored items that must be dried, moved or disposed of.
  • Any rebuilding of the subfloor or platform beneath the tank ahead of the new heater.
  • How much insulation, ceiling or wall drywall needs to be taken out.
  • Time that passed with the tank still refilling, up to the point the supply was shut.

What We Do

How We Handle Water Heater Leak Cleanup

1

Extract the Water

We remove standing water from the floor, closet or garage and, for attic units, from the overflow pan and ceiling cavity, so the surrounding materials stop absorbing it.

2

Check Around the Heater

To learn the full reach of the water, our crew meters and scans the platform, the bottoms of the walls, neighboring rooms and the ceiling below if there is one.

3

Remove Wet Material

Soaked insulation, crumbling drywall and damaged baseboards are taken out. For an attic leak, the wet insulation is cleared out, which lets us dry the drywall and ceiling joists beneath.

4

Drying and Moisture Readings

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. We record moisture readings and tell you when the space is ready for the new heater.
