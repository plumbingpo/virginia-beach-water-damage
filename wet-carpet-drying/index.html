Wet Carpet Drying: What Virginia Beach Homeowners Should Know

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. What you can pull out with towels or a shop vac comes from the top, not from below. In Virginia Beach, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Two things decide if a carpet is worth saving: the cleanliness of the water and the time it has sat wet. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

What It Looks Like

  • Yellow or brown discoloration rising to the fiber tips while the carpet dries
  • Loose, wavy sections where the tack strip no longer holds the carpet
  • Dampness that spreads outward on its own, well after the leak was stopped
  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or leaves your socks damp when you walk on it
  • Backing that separates from the carpet when you lift a corner to look
  • Wet tack strip showing through as dark, rusty streaks next to the baseboard
Save or Replace

Dry It or Replace It?

It depends on the material, how long it stayed wet and how clean the water was. We test before we decide, and we tell you why.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Edges

We trace how far the water traveled with moisture meters and thermal imaging, including under walls and into closets. The water category gets identified as well, since it determines what we can keep.

2

Extract the Water

We use weighted extraction tools that squeeze water from carpet and pad at once. A wand by itself leaves much more behind, so this step cuts the drying time afterward.

3

Dry Every Layer

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until carpet and subfloor both read dry.

4

Clean and Reset

We treat the carpet and subfloor with an antimicrobial where needed, lay new pad if the old one came out, restretch the carpet onto the tack strip, and clean it.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Furniture Marks

When carpet is wet, the finish on wooden legs and the rust from metal glides transfer into it. Such spots are frequently there for good, while the legs soak up water and swell.

Mold Under the Carpet

The dark, damp underside of the carpet is where mold usually begins. You smell it long before you ever see it on top.

Water Quality Drops

Water that started clean does not stay that way. As it sits in carpet and pad it picks up soil and bacteria, so a carpet that could have been dried may have to be removed.

Soaked Pad Underneath

A carpet pad absorbs many times what it weighs in water. It keeps feeding moisture into the carpet above and the floor below long after the surface feels dry.

If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Water Spreads Sideways

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. The tack strip starts taking on water as it creeps below the walls toward the room next door.

Hours 24 to 48

Odor and Mold Begin

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Dust held down in the fibers and the carpet backing both give mold a place to begin.

Day 3 to 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. The subfloor swells and the tack strip nails begin to rust.

Past one week

Past Saving

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.
