How to Tell You Need Emergency Water Damage Restoration

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Nobody was home when it started, so the length of the leak is unknown.
  • A growing pool on the floor is heading for the next room or running downstairs.
  • The breaker box, a running appliance or cords along the floor sit in the path of the water.
  • Walls on the lower floor are wet from top to bottom because of a leak overhead.
  • Carpet, drywall or hardwood has been soaked for more than a few hours.
  • The water is from a sewer backup or looks dark and smells bad.
  • An outlet, a light fixture or the ceiling above has water dripping out of it.

How to Prevent Emergency Water Damage Restoration Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Any trip beyond a few days is a reason to turn the main supply off before you go.
  • Swap worn hoses feeding the washer, sinks and toilets rather than waiting for a rupture.
  • Before anything goes wrong, store the phone number for an emergency restoration crew.
  • A sump pump should be tried out before storms arrive, ideally with a backup battery installed.
  • Important paperwork and valuables belong above floor level, most of all on the lowest story.
  • Learn the location of your breaker panel, which lets you safely kill power to a wet room.
