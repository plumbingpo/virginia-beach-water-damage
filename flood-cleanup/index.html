Floods start in many ways: heavy rain, storm surge, a river over its banks, a dead sump pump, a sewer backup or a major plumbing failure, and all of them call for fast professional help. Floodwater is almost always rated Category 2 (gray water) or Category 3 (black water), so it carries bacteria, contaminants and possibly hazardous material that can make people seriously ill. For flood emergencies anywhere in Virginia Beach, Virginia Beach Restoration is available 24/7 to extract the water, disinfect affected areas and bring the property back safely.

Cleanup opens with rapid extraction: truck-mounted units and submersible pumps that pull thousands of gallons an hour out of the building. With the standing water gone, our IICRC-certified technicians check the contamination level and apply the right sanitizing steps, including antimicrobial treatment, HEPA air filtration and disinfection of every affected surface. Anything unsalvageable comes out and is hauled off correctly, including wet insulation, drywall that took in contaminated water and waterlogged carpet padding.

Once the space is extracted and sanitized, our team brings in commercial drying gear, namely low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers plus high-velocity air movers, so the structural components dry all the way through. We check moisture levels every day with calibrated meters and thermal imaging so the drying is complete and mold does not get a start. From start to finish we document each step for your insurance claim, with the moisture maps, equipment logs, photos and scope-of-work reports adjusters ask for.

Flood Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Seal foundation cracks and apply a waterproofing coating to basement walls
  • Check that grading on all sides of the house carries water away from the foundation
  • Keep documents and keepsakes in watertight bins on shelves, not on a basement floor
  • A backwater valve on your sewer line keeps a backup from pushing sewage indoors
  • Add a sump pump with battery backup, since power outages and storms tend to arrive together
  • Extend your downspouts to carry rain from the roof out past the foundation

What a Professional Brings to Flood Cleanup

Getting the standing water out is only one part of cleaning up after a flood. Floodwater carries bacteria, sewage, chemicals and other contaminants that are serious health hazards. A trained cleanup team knows how to set containment, wear PPE (personal protective equipment) correctly and sanitize by protocol, which protects your household from hepatitis, E. coli and other diseases carried in water.

Beyond the health concerns, a poor flood cleanup often leaves hidden moisture that turns into severe mold growth within days. Professional technicians use moisture meters, thermal imaging and psychrometric calculations to make sure every affected area, including wall cavities, subfloors and framing, reaches an acceptable drying standard. Nothing else reliably heads off secondary damage, and that damage can run thousands more to remediate, so we dry by the numbers.

How to Tell You Need Flood Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Living areas flooded by contaminated water from a backed-up drain or sewer
  • Storm damage that lets rain in through the roof or walls
  • Basement flooding after the sump pump quit in a downpour
  • Soft, wet drywall or carpeting that is soaked through
  • Soaked landscaping that leaves water pooling against your foundation
  • Tide marks on drywall or trim where the water peaked
  • Appliances such as a water heater, washer or dishwasher spilling water across the floor
