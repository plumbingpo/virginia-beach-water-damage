Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Virginia Beach

Sump pumps do a single task, yet they commonly quit right when that task matters. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. Water rises over the rim of the pit and spreads across the basement floor. Until pumping resumes, the water continues to rise.

This water is not clean. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. That changes the cleanup: porous materials like carpet pad and wet drywall usually come out, and everything left is cleaned and disinfected before drying starts.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Keep Out of the BasementWith the electricity still on, a flooded basement is not safe to walk into. Water may be touching outlets, the furnace or the pump's own cord.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsBasement breakers should be flipped off, provided you can get to the panel without standing in water. Call the utility when the panel itself sits in the flooded area.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Do not reach into the water.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedChildren and pets stay on the upper floor, anyone who has to enter wears rubber boots and gloves, and hands get washed after contact with anything wet.
  5. Move Valuables and CallGet dry valuables to a higher floor, snap photos showing the pump and how high the water is, and give us a call. Holding off until the basement empties is not the answer.
Coverage

Insurance and Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

This is one of the most misunderstood claims. Standard homeowners coverage generally leaves out sump overflow and backup, while flood coverage is bought separately and follows other rules. Unless you bought an endorsement for sump overflow or water backup there is often no coverage, and such endorsements are capped. Check the declarations page for it, or call your agent to find out. To keep the claim file complete, our team documents the damage, the failed pump and the height of the water.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

1

Get the Water Out

Our crew arrives with extractors and pumps, which means your dead sump is not needed. Standing water and silt are removed from the floor, the pit area and any low spots.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Carpet, padding, insulation and wet drywall that the water contacted are pulled by a crew in protective gear, bagged and removed from the property so the concrete and studs are open.

3

Wash and Disinfect

An antimicrobial product goes on after the concrete, the studs and any kept belongings have been washed. Drying comes afterward, because fans on a dirty basement would blow contaminants everywhere.

We run drying equipment until our meters show normal moisture in the slab and the wood, which typically takes 3 to 5 days. Before we pull our equipment, the pit should have a pump that works.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Dirt and small rocks washed into the basin that lock up the pump.
  • A discharge line that is blocked, crushed or disconnected outside the house.
  • Losing electricity in a storm when the pump has no battery backup.
  • The pump being unplugged by accident or sitting on an outlet that tripped.
  • An old motor that burned out after years of cycling on and off.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Bottom Plates and Studs

Framing lumber at floor level takes on groundwater before anything else in the wall. With the wall opened up, this wood is scrubbed, treated and then dried to normal readings.

Drywall and Wall Insulation

Dirty water climbs from the slab into insulation and drywall, far higher than the line you can see. Since it is contaminated water, we remove the wet bottom portion instead of drying it.

Carpet and Flooring

Groundwater-soaked carpet and padding come out, as cleaning cannot make them safe. Silty water also gets caught below vinyl plank and laminate, which normally means lifting them.

Boxes and Stored Items

Books, cloth, boxes and wood furniture legs take in dirty water within a short time. Porous items left sitting in the water resist disinfecting and grow mold in a matter of days.
